1

Listen in

5 min · pens down

Your teacher reads the conversation aloud. Just listen and follow along. Don't mark anything yet — listen for the meaning. Who are these two people? What do they want?

Amara

Hi — I don't think we've met. I'm Amara Diallo.

Marco

Marco Reyes. Nice to meet you. Are you with the healthcare panel?

Amara

No — I'm here through MassHire. I work in pharmacy. You?

Marco

Engineering. Civil. Though right now I'm in construction while I requalify.

Amara

That's interesting. How long does requalification take?

Marco

About two years for the full exam. I'm a year in.

Amara

That takes real commitment. I'm doing something similar — US certification.

Marco

Which field?

Amara

Pharmacy. PharmD equivalent. It's… a process.

Marco

(laughs) Everything here is a process.

Amara

Exactly. Look — I'd love to connect on LinkedIn. Would that be okay?

Marco

Absolutely. Here's my card.

Amara

Great. It was really good to meet you. I hope the requalification goes well.

Marco

You too. Good luck with the certification.

Amara

Thank you.

Quick meaning check

Answer in a few words. Compare with a partner.

  1. What work is Marco doing now, and what work does he want to return to?
  2. What do Amara and Marco have in common?
  3. Who asks to stay in touch — and how do they ask?
2

Notice it

10 min · pens up

Now read the conversation again while your teacher reads it a second time. This time, think about how it sounds. On the transcript above, mark the stressed syllables (CAPS), the pauses (/) and the intonation (↗ ↘). Then answer the questions.

  1. Amara says “That takes real commitment.” She is showing respect for Marco's effort.

    Circle the intonation you hear: ↗ rising  /  ↘ falling

    Now say it the other way. What changes? Does it still sound like a compliment?

  2. Marco says “Everything here is a process.” He is not only talking about himself — he is talking about a shared experience.

    Why does this sentence bring the two speakers closer together?

    Does his voice rise ↗ or fall ↘? Why?

  3. Amara closes with “I'd love to connect on LinkedIn. Would that be okay? ↗” The rise makes the request softer.

    Compare it with: “Let's connect on LinkedIn. ↘”

    How does each one feel to the listener? When might the second one be fine?

3

Pronunciation focus: real interest vs. polite performance

10 min

Prosody is the music of your voice: stress, rhythm, pauses and pitch. Small talk works when your prosody says I am actually interested in you — not just I am being polite. In English, three things change when interest is real:

  1. The question rises higher at the end. ↗
  2. Short responses come quickly: “That's interesting.” “Really?” “Oh, wow.”
  3. The voice gets a little softer and slower — closer, not louder.

The difference is small. But people feel it immediately.

Examples from the conversation — listen, then mark them yourself.
Marked exampleWhat to notice
How long does requalification take? ↗The rise says the question is real, not automatic.
That takes real commitment. ↘Falling. It's a statement that lands — not a question.
I'd love to connect on LinkedIn. / Would that be okay? ↗Statement falls, then the check rises and softens the request.

Two things to watch

  • Flat voice. If your pitch never moves, a friendly question can sound bored or even rude.
  • Everything rising. If every sentence goes up ↗, you sound unsure of yourself. Statements fall ↘. Questions and soft requests rise ↗.

Say it

8 min · pairs

Mark the stressed syllables in CAPITALS and add / where you pause. The first line is done for you. Then say each line to your partner.

  • NICE to MEET you. / What BRINGS you HERE? ↗example
  • That's interesting. How long have you been in that field? ↗
  • That takes real commitment. ↘
  • Everything here is a process. ↘
  • I'd love to stay in touch. Would that be okay? ↗

Partner game: believe me or not

Say each line twice — once sounding bored, once sounding genuinely interested. Don't tell your partner which is which. Your partner says: “I believe you” or “I don't believe you.” Then swap roles.

Speaking task A: networking event

15 min · stand up

Introduce yourself to three people you have not spoken to today. With each person, learn one real thing and share one real thing. Fill in the table after each conversation, not during it.

Pocket phrases

Open

  • Hi — I don't think we've met. I'm ___.
  • Are you here with ___?
  • What brings you here? ↗

Ask about their work

  • What field are you in? ↗
  • How long have you been doing that? ↗
  • What's the hardest part? ↗

Show interest

  • That's interesting. ↘
  • Really? ↗
  • That takes real commitment. ↘
  • Everything here is a process. ↘

Close politely

  • It was really good to meet you. ↘
  • I won't keep you — enjoy the rest of the event.
  • I hope the exam goes well. ↘

6

Speaking task B: a connection that mattered

2 min think · 3 min each

Tell your partner about a professional or personal connection you made in America that was unexpected or important.

  • Who did you meet, and where?
  • What did you talk about?
  • What made the connection work — what did they say, or what did you say?
  • Has it led anywhere? Or is it important for another reason?

Listener, you have a job too

  • Ask at least two follow-up questions. Start one with “How long…” and one with “What made…”
  • Use a short response at the right moment: “Really?” “That's interesting.” “That takes commitment.”
  • Watch your partner's face. Did your question sound interested, or automatic?

Now write your own opening line for a networking conversation — the way you would really say it. Mark the stress and the intonation.

7

Listen and respond

10 min

Your teacher reads this welcome speech. Take notes while you listen — key words only, not full sentences. Then answer in full sentences.

Host

Welcome, everyone. We have about forty-five minutes before the formal session begins.

Host

I always think this is actually the most important part of any professional event.

Host

The people in this room represent more than thirty countries. / More than twenty professional fields.

Host

And many of you have had to rebuild your career — / your professional identity — / in a new language.

Host

That is not a deficit. / That is a superpower.

Host

So — / please introduce yourselves. / Be curious. / Ask real questions.

Host

The person next to you may be your next colleague, / your next mentor, / or the person who tells you about the opportunity / you didn't know existed.

Host

Enjoy the time.

My notes

  1. What does the host say about the forty-five minutes before the formal session? Why does he think that?
  2. Look at: “That is not a deficit. / That is a superpower.”

    a. What does that refer to?

    b. The two sentences have the same shape. What does the repetition do to the listener?

    c. Which two words does the host stress most? Mark them in CAPITALS above.

  3. Write one question you would ask someone at this event, based on what the host said. Mark the intonation.

Words from this unit

requalify — to take exams again so you can do your profession in a new country
certification — official proof that you are qualified
a deficit — something missing; a weakness
a mentor — an experienced person who guides you
to stay in touch — to keep contact after you meet
“It's a process.” — it's long and complicated (said with patience, often humour)
