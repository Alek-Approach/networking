2

Notice it

10 min · pens up

Your teacher reads the conversation a second time. Now think about how it sounds. Go back up to the conversation and mark the stress, the pauses and the intonation. Then answer the questions.

  1. Amara says “That takes real commitment.” She is showing respect for Marco's effort.

    Which intonation do you hear?

    Now say it the other way. What changes? Does it still sound like a compliment?

  2. Marco says “Everything here is a process.” He is not only talking about himself — he is talking about a shared experience.

    Why does this sentence bring the two speakers closer together? Does his voice rise ↗ or fall ↘, and why?

  3. Amara closes with “I'd love to connect on LinkedIn. Would that be okay? ↗” The rise makes the request softer.

    Compare it with: “Let's connect on LinkedIn. ↘”

    How does each one feel to the listener? When might the second one be fine?

3

Pronunciation focus: real interest vs. polite performance

10 min

Prosody is the music of your voice: stress, rhythm, pauses and pitch. Small talk works when your prosody says I am actually interested in you — not just I am being polite. In English, three things change when interest is real:

  1. The question rises higher at the end. ↗
  2. Short responses come quickly: “That's interesting.” “Really?” “Oh, wow.”
  3. The voice gets a little softer and slower — closer, not louder.

The difference is small. But people feel it immediately.

Examples from the conversation — listen, then say them yourself.
Marked exampleWhat to notice
How long does requalification take? ↗The rise says the question is real, not automatic.
That takes real commitment. ↘Falling. It's a statement that lands — not a question.
I'd love to connect on LinkedIn. / Would that be okay? ↗Statement falls, then the check rises and softens the request.

Two things to watch

  • Flat voice. If your pitch never moves, a friendly question can sound bored or even rude.
  • Everything rising. If every sentence goes up ↗, you sound unsure of yourself. Statements fall ↘. Questions and soft requests rise ↗.

Speaking task B: a connection that mattered

2 min think · 3 min each

Tell your partner about a professional or personal connection you made in America that was unexpected or important.

  • Who did you meet, and where?
  • What did you talk about?
  • What made the connection work — what did they say, or what did you say?
  • Has it led anywhere? Or is it important for another reason?

Listener, you have a job too

  • Ask at least two follow-up questions. Start one with “How long…” and one with “What made…”
  • Use a short response at the right moment: “Really?” “That's interesting.” “That takes commitment.”
  • Watch your partner's face. Did your question sound interested, or automatic?
